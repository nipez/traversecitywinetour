Best Lake View Wineries on the Traverse City Wine Trail

9 min read  ·  August 2026  ·  Traverse City Wine Country Journal
Home› Journal› Best Lake View Wineries

Traverse City wine country sits between two arms of Grand Traverse Bay and a chain of inland lakes on Leelanau. That geography is why so many tasting rooms pour with a water view — and why “wineries with lake views” is one of the searches visitors make before they book a trip.

This guide ranks the Traverse City wine trail stops where the view is part of the tasting: ridge-top estates on Old Mission Peninsula looking over East and West Bay, and Leelanau hilltops above Lake Leelanau. Use it to build a day that pairs serious wine with the scenery that makes Michigan wine country feel unlike anywhere else.

Tip: Clear days after a cold front give the sharpest bay views. Midweek mornings are quieter if you want photos without a crowd at the rail.

Why Traverse City Wineries Have Such Good Views

Old Mission Peninsula is a narrow ridge between East and West Grand Traverse Bay. Vineyards climb both sides, so many tasting rooms sit high enough to see water in one or both directions. Leelanau’s interior hills rise above Lake Leelanau, so estates like Bel Lago and Forty-Five North trade bay panoramas for lake-and-vineyard decks.

Water does more than look good. Grand Traverse Bay and Lake Michigan moderate frost risk and stretch the growing season — the same climate that makes Riesling and sparkling wine work here also put tasting decks on the best ridgelines in northern Michigan.

Old Mission Peninsula: Bay Views

Start on Old Mission if you want the classic “vineyards dropping to blue water” Traverse City wine trail experience. These five stops are the strongest lake- and bay-view tasting rooms on the peninsula.

Old Mission Peninsula · 360° Bay Views

Chateau Chantal

Chateau Chantal sits on one of the highest points on Old Mission, with views of both East and West Grand Traverse Bay. The European-style estate, terrace, and on-site B&B make it a first stop for visitors who want the postcard Traverse City winery experience. Summer Jazz at Sunset on the terrace is built around that view.

Must try: Estate sparkling, Pinot Grigio · Best for: First visit, sunset terrace

Old Mission Peninsula · Tip of the Peninsula

2 Lads Winery

Near the tip of Old Mission, 2 Lads earns “best view of any tasting room” comments for a reason: a contemporary pour room looking over vineyards to Grand Traverse Bay. Small-lot Cabernet Franc and Pinot Noir pair well with a longer stop if you only have time for a few bay-view wineries.

Must try: Cabernet Franc, Grüner Veltliner · Best for: Photo stop + curated tasting

Old Mission Peninsula · East Bay Panorama

Brys Estate Vineyard & Winery

Brys Estate’s modern tasting room uses floor-to-ceiling glass facing East Grand Traverse Bay. The architecture frames the water as clearly as any winery on the trail, and the wine list (Signature Red, Naked Chardonnay, plus a distillery) gives groups something for every palate.

Must try: Signature Red, Naked Chardonnay · Best for: Modern tasting room + bay light

Old Mission Peninsula · Ridge Above Peninsula Drive

Hawthorne Vineyards

Hawthorne occupies a dramatic ridge above Peninsula Drive with sweeping views over both bays. It is a quieter alternative to the bigger Old Mission estates, and a strong pick if you want the view without a packed patio on peak summer weekends.

Must try: Estate whites and cool-climate reds · Best for: Quiet ridge-top tasting

Old Mission Peninsula · Pioneer Estate Views

Chateau Grand Traverse

Michigan’s pioneering Riesling house still delivers panoramic bay and vineyard views from the tasting room. Pair a dry Riesling flight with a walk of the grounds — CGT is the historical anchor of Old Mission and still one of the most photogenic stops on a lake-view wine day.

Must try: Dry Riesling, ice wine when available · Best for: Classic TC wine country views

Leelanau Peninsula: Lake Leelanau Views

Cross to Leelanau for inland lake vistas and a different tasting-room pace. These three wineries put Lake Leelanau (or open Leelanau farmland that feels like it) front and center.

Leelanau Peninsula · “Beautiful Lake”

Bel Lago Vineyard & Winery

Bel Lago — Italian for “beautiful lake” — sits on a bluff above Lake Leelanau. The outdoor deck overlooking water and vines is one of the best afternoon pour spots in Michigan wine country. Estate Auxerrois, Pinot Noir, and Gewürztraminer reward staying for a second glass.

Must try: Auxerrois, Pinot Noir · Best for: Lake deck afternoon

Leelanau Peninsula · 45th Parallel Hilltop

Forty-Five North

Named for the latitude that runs through the property, Forty-Five North sits on a hilltop with sweeping Lake Leelanau and countryside views. Large windows and an outdoor deck make the setting as memorable as the bold Cabernet Franc and red blends the winery is known for.

Must try: Cabernet Franc, Pinot Blanc · Best for: Red-wine fans who want a lake view

Leelanau Peninsula · Suttons Bay Hillside

Willow Vineyard

Willow’s wind-swept Suttons Bay hillside looks toward West Bay — a quieter, estate-driven stop with Pinot Noir and Chardonnay that fits after a morning on Old Mission if you still want water in the frame.

Must try: Estate Pinot Noir, Chardonnay · Best for: Intimate West Bay hillside stop

A One-Day Lake-View Wine Itinerary

You can chase views on both peninsulas in one day if you keep the stop list tight. Here is a realistic route that prioritizes water sightlines without rushing every pour.

  • Morning (Old Mission): Chateau Chantal for both-bay context, then 2 Lads for the tip-of-peninsula panorama.
  • Midday: Brys Estate for East Bay glass and lunch nearby, or picnic if the winery allows that day.
  • Afternoon (Leelanau): Drive west to Bel Lago’s Lake Leelanau deck; finish at Forty-Five North if you want a second lake hilltop and stronger reds.
  • Skip if short on time: Hawthorne and Willow — excellent views, easier to save for a second day.

Designated driver or tour?

Bay-view days feel longer than they look on a map. A private Traverse City wine tour keeps everyone tasting and puts the ridge roads in someone else’s hands — especially valuable between Old Mission and Leelanau.

When to Visit for the Best Views

Late spring – early summer

Fresh green vines, long light, and clearer bay horizons before peak July crowds. Ideal for terrace photos.

September – October

Harvest color in the vineyards with deep blue water behind them. Book ahead; harvest weekends fill fast.

Clear cold fronts

After a front passes, visibility over Grand Traverse Bay can stretch for miles. Worth watching the forecast.

Winter

Fewer decks are open, but snow-lined ridges above the bay are dramatic. Check seasonal hours before you go.

Planning Tips for View-Focused Tastings

  • Arrive at popular Old Mission stops (Chateau Chantal, Brys, 2 Lads) near opening on summer Saturdays.
  • Confirm outdoor seating and weather — many of the best views are patio or terrace pours.
  • Pair this list with our Old Mission vs. Leelanau guide if you can only do one peninsula.
  • Use the interactive winery map to sequence stops without backtracking on Peninsula Drive or M-22.

Plan Your Day

Traverse City’s wine trail is one of the few American wine regions where “best tasting rooms” and “best lake views” often mean the same places. Start with Chateau Chantal and 2 Lads on Old Mission, finish with Bel Lago on Lake Leelanau, and you will understand why visitors plan whole weekends around the water as much as the wine.

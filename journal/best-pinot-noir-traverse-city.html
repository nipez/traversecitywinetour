Best Pinot Noir in Traverse City Wine Country

8 min read  ·  September 2026  ·  Traverse City Wine Country Journal
Home› Journal› Best Pinot Noir in Traverse City

Traverse City is famous for Riesling — and for good reason. But if you taste only whites, you miss one of Michigan wine country’s quieter wins: cool-climate Pinot Noir that leans elegant and earthy rather than heavy or jammy.

This guide points you to the best Pinot Noir tasting rooms on Old Mission and Leelanau Peninsulas. Use it when you want a red-focused Traverse City wine trail day, or when someone in the group asks for something beyond sparkling and Riesling.

Pinot Noir here is typically lighter in body, higher in acidity, and more savory than California styles. Ask for current and library pours when available — vintage variation is part of the story.

Why Pinot Noir Works in Traverse City

Pinot Noir thrives where summers are long enough to ripen but cool enough to keep acid and aromatics. Old Mission and Leelanau sit near the 45th parallel, moderated by Grand Traverse Bay and Lake Michigan. That thermal buffer is why the same vineyards that make award-winning Riesling can also produce Pinot with cherry, cranberry, forest-floor, and spice notes without tipping into overripe fruit.

Michigan Pinot will not taste like Willamette Valley or Burgundy copycats. The best bottles show place: bright red fruit, firm acidity, and a finish that pairs easily with lake fish, roasted chicken, or a cheese board after the tasting room.

Leelanau Peninsula Pinot Noir Stops

Leelanau’s hills and inland lakes reward a Pinot-focused afternoon. These estates consistently put Pinot Noir near the center of the pour list.

Leelanau Peninsula · Fieldstone Estate

Shady Lane Cellars

Shady Lane is a first stop for Pinot hunters. The restored 1800s fieldstone tasting room matches an elegant, cool-climate style — expressive Pinot Noir alongside the Rieslings the winery is known for. Minimal-intervention winemaking keeps the fruit clear and site-driven.

Must try: Estate Pinot Noir · Best for: Benchmark Leelanau Pinot

Leelanau Peninsula · Lake Leelanau Bluff

Bel Lago Vineyard & Winery

Bel Lago’s bluff above Lake Leelanau is worth the drive on its own. Estate-grown Pinot Noir sits beside the winery’s rare Auxerrois and Gewürztraminer — a strong stop if you want reds and aromatic whites in one flight with a lake deck afterward.

Must try: Pinot Noir, Auxerrois · Best for: Pinot + lake views

Leelanau Peninsula · Northport Area

Gill's Pier Vineyard & Winery

Small-lot Pinot Noir from Gill’s Pier tends toward delicate and earthy — the kind of bottle that rewards tasting room conversation more than a rushed flight. Production is limited, so Pinot can sell through; call ahead if it is your must-pour.

Must try: Pinot Noir, Riesling · Best for: Intimate, limited Pinot

Leelanau Peninsula · Leland / Fishtown

Verterra Winery

Verterra’s Leland tasting room makes it easy to fold Pinot into a Fishtown afternoon. The portfolio emphasizes terroir-driven Leelanau fruit, with elegant Pinot Noir alongside aromatic whites and the occasional experimental pour.

Must try: Pinot Noir, Grüner Veltliner · Best for: Village stop + Pinot

Leelanau Peninsula · Suttons Bay Hillside

Willow Vineyard

Willow’s hillside estate Pinot Noir and Chardonnay are a quieter counterpoint to bigger Leelanau rooms. West Bay proximity and a smaller pour list make it a strong closer if you want one more Pinot without another crowded patio.

Must try: Estate Pinot Noir · Best for: Quiet hillside Pinot

Old Mission Peninsula Pinot Noir Stops

Old Mission Pinot often shares the flight with Cabernet Franc and Chardonnay. These rooms are the most reliable for visitors who want reds without leaving the peninsula.

Old Mission Peninsula · Tip of the Peninsula

2 Lads Winery

2 Lads pours small-lot Pinot Noir with the depth cool-climate red drinkers look for, in a modern tasting room overlooking Grand Traverse Bay. Pair it with their Cabernet Franc if you want a side-by-side of Old Mission’s two strongest red stories.

Must try: Pinot Noir, Cabernet Franc · Best for: Serious reds + bay views

Old Mission Peninsula · Boutique Estate

Bonobo Winery

Bonobo’s Pinot Noir and Chardonnay lean Burgundian in spirit — elegant, limited production, and poured in an intimate room. Ideal if your group prefers a slower tasting over a high-volume estate stop.

Must try: Pinot Noir, Chardonnay · Best for: Small-batch Pinot

Old Mission Peninsula · Estate & B&B

Chateau Chantal

Chateau Chantal’s Pinot Noir sits within a broad estate portfolio, but the ridge-top setting and both-bay views make it an easy first or last stop on a Pinot-curious Old Mission day. Ask what vintage is pouring — warmer years show riper fruit; cooler years emphasize spice and acid.

Must try: Pinot Noir, estate sparkling · Best for: First visit + Pinot option

A Pinot-Focused Half-Day Route

  • Leelanau focus (half day): Shady Lane → Bel Lago → Willow. Add Gill’s Pier if you are already heading toward Northport.
  • Old Mission focus (half day): Bonobo → 2 Lads → Chateau Chantal (or reverse for sunset at Chantal).
  • Both peninsulas (full day): Morning at 2 Lads and Bonobo; afternoon at Shady Lane and Bel Lago. Keep lunch flexible — Pinot flights add up.

Still want whites in the mix?

Pair this route with our best Rieslings guide or the Michigan Riesling story. Many of these same rooms pour both — just ask for a red-leaning flight.

What to Expect in the Glass

Typical profile

Red cherry, cranberry, tea leaf, light spice; medium-minus body; bright acid.

Food matches

Lake whitefish, mushroom pasta, roasted poultry, mild soft cheeses.

Best season to taste

Fall and winter rooms highlight Pinot; summer works if you start with lighter pours.

Buying tip

Ask about library bottles and club allocations — Pinot lots sell out faster than Riesling.

Planning Tips

  • Confirm Pinot is on the current tasting menu before you drive to limited-production rooms like Gill’s Pier.
  • Designate a driver or book a Traverse City wine tour — red flights are easy to over-pour.
  • Use the winery map to avoid backtracking between Suttons Bay, Lake Leelanau, and Old Mission.
  • If you can only do one peninsula, choose Leelanau for Pinot density or Old Mission for Pinot plus bay-view tasting rooms.

Build a Pinot Noir Tasting Day

Pin these wineries on the interactive planner and sequence Old Mission or Leelanau stops without rushing the pour.

Plan Your Day

Traverse City Pinot Noir will not shout. The best bottles reward a slower sip — bright fruit, northern acidity, and a finish that makes the next tasting room easy to justify. Start at Shady Lane or 2 Lads, then follow the peninsula that fits your day.

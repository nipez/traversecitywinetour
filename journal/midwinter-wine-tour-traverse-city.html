Midwinter Wine Tour in Traverse City: How to Plan a January Day

~11 min read  ·  November 2026  ·  Traverse City Wine Country Journal
Home› Journal› Midwinter Wine Tour Guide

By mid-January, Traverse City wine country is deep into its quiet season — snow on the vines, shorter tasting-room windows, and fewer walk-in crowds than July. If you are planning a midwinter wine tour six to eight weeks out, this guide is the practical layer: how January hours typically behave, which peninsula fits a short day, and how to confirm stops without inventing a schedule.

For the broader cold-season overview, start with our Traverse City wineries winter guide and the more atmospheric winter wine trail. This article zooms in on January planning after the holidays.

We do not invent winter hours. Schedules change with weather, staffing, and holidays — check each winery's site and our Open this week picker before you leave downtown.

What midwinter tasting rooms are like

Winter does not close Old Mission or Leelanau. What changes is the shape of the day:

  • Many rooms run shorter daily windows or lean weekend-heavy compared with summer
  • Daylight ends early, so two solid pours beat five rushed ones
  • Staff often have more time for conversation when rooms are quiet
  • Road conditions can erase a stop — build buffer, not a minute-by-minute grid

Recent venue research for the 2025–2026 winter season found a core set of estates publishing year-round or winter schedules, plus others that ask guests to call ahead. Treat every published window as a planning cue, then reconfirm the morning of your tour.

Old Mission vs Leelanau in January

Old Mission Peninsula

Shorter hops and closer to downtown lodging — the lower-risk midwinter default when forecast or daylight looks tight.

Leelanau Peninsula

More distance between estates. Worth it on a clear Saturday with two or three confirmed rooms, not a summer-length checklist.

January shortlist anchors

These stops appear often in winter routes because they publicly maintain cold-season presence. That is not a promise they will be open on your date — verify first.

Old Mission Peninsula

Chateau Grand Traverse

A peninsula landmark that publishes seasonal schedules including a winter window. Strong first or last stop when bay views and Riesling are on the list.

Old Mission Peninsula

Peninsula Cellars

The schoolhouse tasting room is a classic cold-weather favorite when it is pouring. Their published calendar also notes major holiday closures — check before New Year week spillover dates.

Leelanau Peninsula

Black Star Farms

Estate setting with wine and cider options — a useful single substantial Leelanau stop when you want quality over hopscotch.

Leelanau Peninsula

L. Mawby

Michigan sparkling that feels festive against snow. Often worth confirming weekend hours before you plan brunch timing around it.

Planning rule

Confirm open status first, map drive order second, book lodging and dinner third. Reverse that order in January and you will waste a Saturday chasing closed doors.

Frequently asked questions

Are Traverse City wineries open in January?

Many tasting rooms on Old Mission and Leelanau stay open through midwinter, but schedules often shrink versus summer. Confirm each stop before you drive — use our Open this week filter and the winery's own site.

How many wineries should I plan for a January tour?

Two to three confirmed stops is realistic. Short daylight and reduced hours make long hopscotch routes fragile.

Is a private wine tour worth it in midwinter?

Often yes when roads are icy or daylight is short. A private driver lets everyone taste and keeps the day flexible if a tasting room changes hours.

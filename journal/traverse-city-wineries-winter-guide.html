Traverse City Wineries in Winter: What's Open and How to Plan Your Tour

~12 min read  ·  October 2026  ·  Traverse City Wine Country Journal
Home› Journal› Traverse City Wineries in Winter Guide

Summer gets the magazine spreads. Winter gets the quieter tasting rooms, snow-lined vineyard rows, and the kind of unhurried pours that are hard to find on a July Saturday. If you are searching for Traverse City wineries in winter — specifically what is open and how to plan a workable tour on Old Mission and Leelanau — this guide is the planning layer, not the romance pitch.

We already covered the atmosphere and ice-wine story in our winter wine trail piece. This article is different: it is a practical seasonal guide for building a cold-weather day (or weekend) on both peninsulas without guessing hours, overpacking the route, or driving to a locked door.

Winter hours change. Treat every schedule below as a planning cue, not a promise — check the winery's site for current hours before you leave downtown.

How winter tasting rooms actually work

Traverse City wine country does not shut down when the leaves drop. A core set of estates on both Old Mission Peninsula and the Leelanau Peninsula keep tasting rooms open through the cold months. What changes is the shape of the day:

  • Many rooms shift to weekend-heavy or shortened daily windows
  • Walk-in energy drops; staff often have more time for conversation
  • Daylight ends earlier, so three solid stops beat five rushed ones
  • Weather can close a road stretch or delay a start — build buffer, not a minute-by-minute grid

Do not invent a summer-density itinerary and hope winter cooperates. Start with the wineries most likely to be pouring, confirm each one the day before (or morning of), then fill gaps only after those confirmations land. For regional context beyond a single weekend, Michigan Vineyards is a useful companion resource for statewide wine-country planning.

Planning rule of thumb

Confirm open status first, map drive order second, book lodging and dinner third. In winter, reverse that order and you will waste a Saturday afternoon chasing closed doors.

Old Mission vs Leelanau in the cold season

Old Mission Peninsula

Shorter hops, closer to downtown lodging, easier to salvage if a storm shortens the day. Best default for a single winter wine afternoon.

Leelanau Peninsula

More scenic distance between estates and a fuller day-trip feel. Ideal when you have daylight, confirmed hours, and patience for rural roads after snow.

If you only have one winter day, Old Mission is usually the lower-risk choice. If you have a long Saturday plus a lighter Sunday, split the peninsulas: Old Mission when the forecast looks messy, Leelanau when skies clear. For a deeper side-by-side, read Old Mission vs Leelanau.

Old Mission winter shortlist

These Old Mission stops are frequent anchors for winter routes because they tend to keep a cold-season presence. Still: check the winery's site for current hours — winter schedules shift with holidays, private events, and weather.

Old Mission Peninsula

Chateau Grand Traverse

A reliable peninsula landmark with bay views that look especially sharp under snow. A strong first or last stop when you want a tasting room that is used to year-round visitors.

Old Mission Peninsula

Chateau Chantal

Ridge-top estate energy with an on-site stay option — useful if you want tasting and lodging without bouncing back to downtown between pours.

Old Mission Peninsula

Bowers Harbor Vineyards

West-bay side stop that pairs well in a compact Old Mission loop. Confirm hours; winter openings here are worth verifying before you build the whole afternoon around it.

Old Mission Peninsula

Brys Estate

Polished tasting room and a natural mid-peninsula connector between southern and northern Old Mission stops when the schedule allows.

Old Mission Peninsula

Peninsula Cellars

The schoolhouse tasting room is a classic cold-weather favorite when it is pouring — intimate space, approachable flights, and a short hop from other peninsula stops.

Leelanau winter shortlist

Leelanau rewards a slower map. Expect more driving between estates and fewer “just drop in” options than in July. Build around two or three confirmed rooms rather than a summer-length checklist.

Leelanau Peninsula

Black Star Farms

Estate setting with wine and cider options — helpful for mixed groups and a natural Leelanau anchor when you want one substantial stop instead of five thin ones.

Leelanau Peninsula

Shady Lane Cellars

Serious cool-climate whites and Pinot in a fieldstone setting that feels made for quiet winter pours. Confirm the day’s hours before you commit the drive.

Leelanau Peninsula

L. Mawby

Michigan sparkling in a tasting room that feels festive against snow and bare vines. Often weekend-leaning in the off-season — verify before you plan brunch timing around it.

Sample Old Mission winter afternoon

Use this as a skeleton, not a locked schedule. Swap order based on confirmed openings and road conditions.

1
Late morning

Leave downtown after coffee

Give yourself a late start if overnight snow is still being cleared. Winter wine days work better when the first stop is confirmed open, not theoretical.

2
Stop 1

Southern or mid-peninsula anchor

Open with a reliable room such as Chateau Grand Traverse or Brys Estate once hours are confirmed.

3
Stop 2

Schoolhouse or harbor-side pour

Peninsula Cellars or Bowers Harbor if open — keep the hop short so weather does not eat the day.

4
Optional Stop 3

Ridge-top closer

Chateau Chantal if daylight and energy remain. Otherwise head downtown for an early dinner while the roads are still easy.

Sample Leelanau winter day

Leelanau needs more intentional sequencing. Confirm two must-stops first, then add a third only if both earlier pours finished early and roads look calm.

1
Morning

Black Star Farms

Use the estate as your northern Leelanau base — wine, cider, and a setting that rewards lingering when the patio season is over.

2
Midday

Shady Lane Cellars

Shift to structured whites and Pinot. Keep lunch flexible: light bites at a tasting room if available, or a planned stop in Suttons Bay / nearby towns.

3
Afternoon

L. Mawby sparkling

Close with bubbles if weekend hours allow. If the room is closed that day, do not force a long detour — trade for downtown wine bars or a second Old Mission stop tomorrow.

How to plan the logistics

  1. Pick one peninsula for Day 1 unless you have a local driver and a clear forecast.
  2. Shortlist 4–5 wineries, then confirm 2–4 that are actually open.
  3. Map drive order north-to-south or south-to-north — avoid crisscrossing the ridge in snow.
  4. Decide whether a private wine tour is worth it for ice, parking, and pacing.
  5. Use Plan Your Day to sanity-check drive times between stops.
  6. Book lodging with easy downtown access so dinner does not become another peninsula slog — see Plan Your Visit for the broader trip checklist.

Clothing and cars matter more than people admit. Waterproof boots for uncleared gravel lots, layers you can shed inside warm tasting rooms, and a vehicle with solid winter tires (or AWD) make the difference between a charming day and a stressful one. If a storm is landing hard, stay in town — winter wine country will still be there on Sunday.

Frequently asked questions

Are Traverse City wineries open in winter?

Many tasting rooms on Old Mission and Leelanau stay open through winter, but schedules often shrink to weekends or shorter daily windows. Always check the winery's site for current hours before you drive.

Should I visit Old Mission or Leelanau for a winter wine day?

Old Mission is usually the easier single-day winter route because stops sit closer together and closer to downtown lodging. Leelanau rewards a full day with more space between estates — confirm hours and build in buffer time for weather.

Do I need a private wine tour in winter?

Not required, but a private driver helps when roads are icy, daylight is short, and tasting-room hours are tighter. It also lets the whole group taste without designating a sober driver.

How many wineries should I plan for a winter day?

Two to four confirmed stops is the sweet spot. Winter daylight and reduced hours make long hopscotch itineraries fragile — quality over quantity.

Winter in Traverse City wine country is not a lesser version of summer. It is a different itinerary problem: fewer open doors, better conversations, and routes that reward confirmation over optimism. Start with the peninsula that fits your weather window, lock the open tasting rooms first, and leave room for the drive home before dark.

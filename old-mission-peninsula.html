Stretching 18 miles into Grand Traverse Bay along the 45th parallel, Old Mission Peninsula is home to some of Michigan's finest wineries. The narrow peninsula's unique microclimate — moderated by water on both sides — produces exceptional Riesling, Pinot Noir, and Chardonnay. Use the guide below, then browse each estate card.

Why Old Mission Peninsula Anchors Traverse City Wine Country

Old Mission Peninsula stretches roughly 18 miles into Grand Traverse Bay along the 45th parallel — the same latitude band often cited alongside classic cool-climate wine regions. Water on both sides moderates the vineyards, which is why Riesling, Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, sparkling wine, and orchard-adjacent specialties thrive here. For many first-time visitors, this peninsula is the clearest one-day introduction to Michigan wine: shorter hops between tasting rooms than Leelanau, and an easy return to downtown lodging.

How to Route Center Road and Peninsula Drive

Most Old Mission days run north from Traverse City along Center Road (M-37), with optional dips to Peninsula Drive for bay-side addresses. Start earlier than you think: several rooms open mid-morning on weekdays, while Sunday hours often start at noon. Build two to four confirmed stops rather than racing eleven doors — quality conversations beat a blur of stickers.

  • Pioneer and panoramic estates: Chateau Grand Traverse (founded 1974; Riesling, ice wine, late-harvest Riesling) and ridge-top Chateau Chantal (sparkling wine, Cerise cherry sparkling, Chardonnay, Pinot Noir) set the classic OMP tone.
  • Modern glass and bay views: Brys Estate brings a contemporary tasting room, signature reds, Naked Chardonnay, and craft spirits context; 2 Lads near the tip emphasizes Cabernet Franc, Pinot Noir, and Grüner with dramatic bay vistas.
  • Approachable and creative: Bowers Harbor Vineyards is known for Langley Chardonnay, rosés, and a welcoming local favorite vibe; Peninsula Cellars pours in an 1896 schoolhouse with Gewürztraminer, Pinot Grigio, cherry wine, and Riesling.
  • Italian hillside and boutique rooms: Mari Vineyards features a large underground cellar and Sangiovese-minded blends; Bonobo Winery keeps a small-batch, intimate pace with Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, and Pinot Meunier.
  • Seasonal and ridge estates: Hawthorne Vineyards lists daily 12–6pm seasonal hours with Riesling and Pinot family wines; Tabone Vineyards is a small-batch estate often open Thu–Sun 12–5pm seasonally — call before you go.
  • Urban OMP producer in town: Left Foot Charley sits at Grand Traverse Commons rather than on Center Road, with Riesling, Blaufränkisch, pét-nat, and cider — a strong downtown coda after peninsula driving.

What the Peninsula Actually Pours

Cool-climate whites lead many flights: Riesling in dry through late-harvest and ice-wine expressions at estates such as Chateau Grand Traverse; Pinot Grigio and Gewürztraminer at Peninsula Cellars and Brys; Chardonnay styles from oak-kissed to “naked” at Brys and Bowers Harbor. Sparkling wine is a Chateau Chantal signature (including Cerise). Reds reward patience in warmer vintages — Pinot Noir at Chantal, Bonobo, and 2 Lads; Cabernet Franc at 2 Lads; Italian-leaning blends at Mari. Fruit wines and cherry specialties still appear because this is cherry country as much as grape country.

Fees and flight menus change. Our winery data records hours and house strengths, not a live price sheet — ask each tasting room for current tasting fees, waive policies, and reservation rules. Peak summer Saturdays book out; shoulder-season weekdays often feel more conversational.

Food, Beer, and Non-Wine Stops on the Same Map

Jolly Pumpkin Restaurant & Brewery on Peninsula Drive is the peninsula's full-service restaurant and artisan brewery — wild-fermented and oak-aged ales plus a real dining room when your group needs lunch between vineyards. After you return to town, Taproot Cider House, Left Foot Charley cider, and downtown brewpubs extend the craft day without another peninsula loop.

If cider is a priority, keep Leelanau orchard stops (Tandem, Suttons Bay Ciders, Bel Lago) for a second day so Old Mission stays focused. The cideries hub and breweries directory help you split the itinerary cleanly.

Sample Day Structures (Adjust to Open Hours)

First-timer compact day: Late-morning flight at Chateau Grand Traverse, lunch or beer at Jolly Pumpkin, afternoon views at Chateau Chantal or Brys Estate, then downtown dinner. Book a driver via wine tours if everyone wants to taste.

Design-and-view day: 2 Lads near the tip, Mari for the underground cellar story, Bowers Harbor for a lighter social stop, optional boutique pour at Bonobo. Confirm tip-of-peninsula drive time and closing hours — several rooms wind down at 5–6pm.

Schoolhouse and seasonal estates: Peninsula Cellars, then call ahead for Hawthorne and Tabone before committing the miles. Finish at Left Foot Charley in the Commons if you want urban natural wine and cider without more Center Road driving.

Tour Guides Versus Self-Drive

Self-drive works when one adult stays sober, parking is available, and you keep the stop count modest. Guided options shine for celebrations, multi-couple groups, and winter roads. Read how to choose a wine tour guide, skim vehicle fits on limo and bus tours and private group tours, then shortlist OMP-savvy operators through Old Mission guides and the guides hub. Deposit, cancellation, and tasting-fee policies belong to each operator — get them in writing.

Weather and season change the peninsula's personality. Summer brings full hours and crowds; fall harvest energy; winter shorter daylight and leaner schedules — pair this page with seasonal journal guidance and always re-check hours on the winery sites or phones listed on each detail page. Frequently Asked Questions

How many wineries are on Old Mission Peninsula?

This guide lists eleven Old Mission–area producers on the map below, from estate pioneers like Chateau Grand Traverse to boutique rooms such as Bonobo and seasonal estates like Tabone and Hawthorne. Always confirm open hours before you drive.

Is Old Mission better than Leelanau for a first visit?

Old Mission is usually the easier first peninsula: shorter drives from downtown Traverse City and tasting rooms clustered along Center Road and Peninsula Drive. Leelanau rewards a longer scenic day — compare both peninsula pages before you choose.

Do I need a wine tour on Old Mission?

Not required, but a private driver helps when your group wants to taste freely. Compare operators on wine tour guides and Old Mission–specific guide pages, then book through wine tours.

Where can I eat or drink beer on Old Mission?

Jolly Pumpkin Restaurant & Brewery on Peninsula Drive is a full-service dining and artisan beer stop among the vineyards. Downtown Traverse City adds more brewery and cidery options after you leave the peninsula.
